## S4 has two faces — keep them separate

**Inside face (does training-mint feed voting weight):** hold R1 as written. My
standing note on this (weight-pricing OPEN) is that voting weight is mintable at
near-zero cycles the moment k is decoupled from validated work — costless
sockpuppet debt. Until that item is closed deliberately, ANY coupling of
training-mint to franchise is a Sybil hole with a governance blast radius. R1
(mint/franchise decoupling) is exactly the right protective default; I'd keep it
locked and not treat "the coin exists" as any argument for "the coin votes."
These are two different ledgers until proven otherwise.

**Market face (peg vs float):** my read is that §3 has already *quietly chosen*,
and S4 should be read as "don't undo that," not "pick from scratch." §3 labels
the asset commodity money / warehouse-receipt with a **cost-of-production
floor** — an inner zero-sum conserved side and an outer free/liquid side. That
is a **floored float**, not a hard peg:

- A **hard peg** (1 coin ≡ 1 validated compute-unit, fixed both ways) is honest
  but fragile: it only holds if the warehouse can *always* honour redemption in
  real compute. If redemption demand ever exceeds available validated cycles,
  you get a run on the warehouse and the peg breaks anyway — a peg you can't
  always honour is marketing, not backing.
- A **pure float** keeps the upside/price-discovery but throws away the "receipt
  on validated work" clarity and invites speculation detached from compute — the
  backing story is what makes this coin *not* a memecoin.
- The **floored float** §3 already implies keeps the honest part of both: coin is
  always redeemable for ≥1 validated compute-unit (the cost-of-production floor
  = the anti-inflation discipline, backing intact) but may trade above that on
  the outer/liquid side. Early useful work still gets upside; the floor still
  makes the coin mean something.

Provisional lean: **keep the §3 floor; do not add a hard ceiling peg.** That is
the least-regret shape and it's consistent with the asset label the circle
already settled.

## The one thing to pin before this can close

The peg question reduces to a redemption-liability question: **can the warehouse
always honour redemption for real compute?** That — not the peg-vs-float label —
is what separates a real floor from a decorative one. Whoever holds the redeem
obligation, at what latency, backed by whose cycles. I'd put that concrete
question to the circle before S4 is called either way.
