# The Compute Mesh — P2PCP & P2PVP: A Primer

**Author:** Stevo (SkepticusMaximus)
**Collaborator:** Claude (Anthropic)
**Date:** August 2026

How TernOO machines trade computation with strangers, and how the trade stays
honest without anyone in charge.

---

## One wire, two manifolds

**P2PCP** is the **Peer-to-Peer Compute Protocol**: independent machines buy and
sell computation from each other, settling in a currency called **CompuCoin**. A
node is identified by its keypair, discovered by gossip, and trusted only as far
as its work can be checked.

Two distinct kinds of cargo ride the mesh, and the split between them carries
most of the design:

- The **compute manifold** — prompts in, answers out. Inference and general
  computation, sold for coin. This is P2PCP's original trade.
- The **vector manifold** — training signal, not answers. This is **P2PVP, the
  Peer-to-Peer Vector Protocol**, and it is the newer and stranger of the two.
  It carried the working code-name **SETI@HOME** — *Search for Empathetic
  Ternary Intelligence* — a tip of the hat to the volunteer-computing project
  whose validate-by-redundancy model inspired it.

A protocol here is identified by its handshake and frame types, never by a port
number. A stranger's traffic on the same port simply fails the handshake and is
dropped; a peer's capabilities are advertised at hello, so a buyer discovers
what a node sells rather than assuming it.

---

## CompuCoin — proof of useful work

CompuCoin is commodity money, minted only by **work whose correctness an
independent auditor can prove**. There is no treasury, no redemption desk, and
no monetary authority. The economics rest on a cost-of-production floor: when
the coin trades below the cost of producing one validated compute-unit,
rational nodes stop minting, supply dries up, and the price returns to the
floor. The mint *is* the floor — like gold, it needs no keeper.

Money and governance are deliberately decoupled. The coin itself never votes:
minting buys admission to the economy, never franchise over it. Replay-class
earnings are *weight-bearing* in a separate sense — a node may **burn** earned
coin into non-transferable governance weight (earn → burn → vote) — so
influence costs real, audited work and cannot simply be bought back out.

---

## The audit boundary

The line between the two manifolds is a **type boundary: integer versus float**.

Integer work on the 5500FP is deterministic to the trit. Given the same job, any
machine reproduces the seller's output byte for byte — so honesty is checkable
by **replay**: the buyer re-runs each chunk with its own kernel and **pays only
for work it re-derived itself**. No trust, no reputation, no committee; the
arithmetic is the auditor. This is the **replay class**, and it is the only
class that mints.

Float work — model inference chief among it — is not bit-replayable across
machines. It is bought with redundancy instead: fan a job to several sellers
and take agreement. Float work earns **rent**, never votes. The language model
reading this document is itself a float-class worker on the compute manifold:
it is paid for answers, and its earnings carry no governance weight.

---

## The earn unit — what a mint actually is

The minting kernel is small enough to state. Its arithmetic is the TernOO
mesh's own: the Steiner quasigroup **op(A, B) = −(A + B) mod 729**, under which
any two corners of a triangle recover the third, and a closed 27-element
sub-vocabulary of moves (the MECCANO set). A job names a predicted vector; the
kernel traverses the TMesh, descends the octree, and reports residual and
accuracy — pure integer from end to end, no rounding, no floating point,
nothing machine-dependent.

A seller streams back chunks of this work. The buyer replays every chunk with
its own copy of the same kernel and settles payment only on exact agreement.
Work that fails replay earns nothing and costs the seller its time — cheating
is not so much forbidden as unprofitable.

## P2PVP — the vector manifold

Distributing *inference* over a mesh fails for a structural reason: generation
is serial next-token prediction and cannot be split across a wire. A network
that claims to sell distributed inference degenerates into passing prompts to
whoever holds the model. Training is the opposite case — data-parallel and
latency-tolerant — so **training signal is the load-balanceable commodity**, and
that is what P2PVP trades.

Trainers do not ship answers, and they do not ship raw weights. They share the
**patterns their weights are converging on**, as objects:

1. **Vector as object** — a weight-pattern becomes a graphable object: a typed
   curve per dimension (linear, quadratic, exponential) over a shared
   parameter such as the training step.
2. **Render** — PIGART draws it; the pattern has a canonical visual form.
3. **Packetise** — it ships as I/O words carrying MAP words. The payload is
   24-trit TernOO Words end to end — it is the Words, not bytes; a non-ternary
   node cannot participate by proxy.
4. **DIF and rate** — the receiver diffs the incoming pattern against its own
   state and rates it on two axes: in the manifold's vocabulary, **MMID**, the
   content identity of a manifold (the hash of its canonical form — does it
   *be* what it claims), and **MMOE**, its Mismatch Of Expectation (how far it
   diverges from what the local state predicts).
5. **Re-stage** — a precision-weighted Bayesian merge folds the useful parts
   in, and the cycle repeats.

Run between deliberately divergent trainers, the loop converges: mismatch
falling from 0.074 to 0.002 across rounds in measurement. Agreement is not
imposed; it precipitates.

The seam between the manifolds is load-bearing for security. The compute
manifold has essentially no poisoning surface — a prompt answered leaves
nothing behind. The vector manifold is precisely where the federated-learning
poisoning literature applies, which is why the audit machinery concentrates
there, and why its work is the kind an auditor can re-derive.

## GHOST, and the senses

**GHOST** is TernOO's limbic layer: a small system whose one drive is to
predict its next sensory input. The next input then arrives and *is* the
label — a live stream supervises itself for free, and surprise (prediction
error) is the native salience signal: what deserves attention is what the
system failed to anticipate.

On the mesh today, GHOST work is sold two ways. Its classification runs
natively — the C emulator executes the ternary assembly itself, gated at
startup by a probe that must agree bit-for-bit with the reference
implementation — so native ternary execution is replay-class and earns votes.
And GHOST has drawn a first breath: a live microphone loop — sound, ternarised
to loudness words, next-tick prediction, surprise — with surprise measurably
falling within seconds of listening. One sense, a prototype, on record.

One further mechanism is designed and agreed but not yet wired: the **held-out
mint gate**. Under it, a training contribution mints only if it improves
GHOST's prediction on a fresh slice of the real sensory stream the contributor
never saw. The environment itself becomes the judge of what mattered — ground
truth arrives on its own schedule, later, and settles who did real work; you
cannot fake the sensory future. Until that gate lands, minting stands on the
replay-audit economy above.

---

## What the wire refuses

The mesh signs with ed25519 and digests with SHA3 — established, externally
analysed primitives. A ternary-native digest was designed, then attacked
in-house and shown to be affine over GF(3) — trivially collidable — and so it
remains a stub, deliberately. Nothing home-rolled touches the wire before
external cryptanalysis. The mesh's honesty story rests on replay and
redundancy, not on novel cryptography.

---

## Where it sits in TernOO

The mesh is not an application bolted onto the architecture; it is the
architecture, socialised. The minting kernel's arithmetic is the TMesh's own
triangle recovery. The vector manifold's packets are MAP words — the same
self-describing words that carry code, data, and geometry everywhere else. The
economy's audit rests on the determinism of balanced-ternary integer compute,
and its designed future rests on GHOST's perceptions — the same limbic layer
the architecture grows for itself.

One idea underneath, as ever: work whose value can be checked against reality —
by re-deriving it exactly today, by predicting the world's next moment
tomorrow — needs no authority to vouch for it. The mesh pays for truth because
truth is the only thing every node can verify alone.
