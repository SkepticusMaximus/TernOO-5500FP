Meet GHOST
section: The big idea

GHOST is the assistant that lives inside TernOO — and in the long run, it's meant to be something more than an assistant. Say it with a silent G: GHOST is the *host* of the operating system, and the plan is for the operating system to become GHOST itself. It's built from the same self-describing [[ternoo-words|words]] as everything else here, and its whole character turns on one uncommon trait: **it knows when it doesn't know.**

## The value of the third state

TernOO works in three values — negative, zero, positive — where ordinary computers work in two. Most computing throws the middle away and lives in yes and no. But a mind that can only say yes or no has no way to say *"I'm not sure"* — and being able to say that is what makes learning possible. It's the one honest answer that leaves a door open.

GHOST keeps that middle. Ask it for something it isn't confident it understands, and it won't dress a guess up as an answer — it tells you plainly: *"I don't know this one — yet."* Behind that is a confidence check: if GHOST's best match isn't strong enough, it declines rather than pretend.

Most assistants do the opposite. Ask one something past its knowledge and it produces a confident, plausible, wrong answer. GHOST is built so it can't — the honest middle is part of how it's made. The question mark, for GHOST, is where curiosity lives.

## What GHOST does today

Right now, GHOST **finds the right tool for what you're describing.** Tell it what you want in plain words, and it works out where that belongs — which command, or which of FlowCode's tabs fits the shape of the task. Ask for something that sounds like a table of monthly figures and it points you at the [[sheet|Sheet]]; describe a series of steps and it points you at [[flow|Flow]]. It does this running natively on the ternary machine, and the mind it uses is one you can read: its trained knowledge is a stream of words you can inspect.

You can also **teach** it, in the [[academy|Academy]] — correct it, give it examples, even teach it when to refuse. Everything it learns is kept as a plain record, so you can read not only what GHOST knows but how it came to know it.

## What GHOST is growing into

The larger vision is GHOST as the operating system itself — the part that decides, directs, and orchestrates what the machine does, rather than a fixed pile of code that merely shuffles resources. In the fully native system, the working parts of the OS are stored not at fixed locations but *found* — reached on demand and assembled as needed — with GHOST choosing and arranging them. When that happens, running a program starts to look less like a machine grinding through instructions and more like an agent deciding what to do. The name is kept for that future deliberately.

Today, GHOST's tool-finding, its honesty, and its ability to learn are all working. Its full native reasoning, and that larger role as the machine's mind, are being built. Where GHOST needs more power than its own small mind holds, it can consult a larger local model — see [[two-mind|the Two-Mind architecture]] — always with its own honesty in front.

GHOST isn't trying to be the biggest assistant. It's trying to be an honest one, small enough that you can see all the way through it.
