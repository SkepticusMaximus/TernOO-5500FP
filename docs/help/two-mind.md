The Two-Mind architecture
section: The big idea

TernOO can work with two very different assistants at once, and the interesting part is how they get along: they cooperate without merging into one.

## Two minds, two jobs

- **GHOST** is small, quick, and honest. You can read its mind — literally, its knowledge is a stream of [[ternoo-words|words]] — and it refuses cleanly when it's unsure. Its strength is that you can trust it, because you can see through it.
- **The Professor** is a large, capable model that can do things GHOST can't. Its strength is sheer ability.

The tempting move would be to fuse them into one bigger, cleverer thing. TernOO doesn't. Instead they talk through a clear hand-off — GHOST out front as the honest face, the Professor behind it as the deep well of ability to draw on when needed.

## How they work together

When GHOST is sure, it just answers — quickly, natively, honestly. When it reaches the edge of what it knows, it can turn to the Professor and ask. But the Professor's answer doesn't reach you raw: it comes back *through* GHOST's honesty first. GHOST never becomes a blind mouthpiece. If the larger model is confidently wrong, the small honest one still has the last word.

This is the project's deepest habit applied to minds: **keep the original.** GHOST stays GHOST; the Professor stays the Professor; neither is dissolved into the other. The small mind keeps the conscience; the large one lends the power; and because both speak the same ternary language, nothing is lost in passing between them.

## Your model, on your machine

The Professor runs entirely on your own computer, as a self-contained program TernOO speaks to directly — with no network connection anywhere in the arrangement. It can't send anything out, because there's nothing in its design that reaches out. When you talk to the Professor, you're talking to your model, on your machine, and the conversation stays there. That guarantee doesn't bend.

## What's ready now

GHOST's part — finding the right tool, refusing honestly, learning what you teach — works today. The hand-off to the Professor is built, and GHOST asks your permission before it consults. The Professor needs its larger model present on your machine, and steps aside gracefully when it isn't there. Some of the deeper cooperation between the two is still being refined, and TernOO is straight about which parts are ready and which are coming.

Two minds: one honest, one powerful, neither pretending to be the other.
